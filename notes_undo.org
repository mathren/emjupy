#+TITLE: Undo in emjupy
#+SUBTITLE: What it does today, why, and what it would take to do better

* The short version

Undo works within a cell, and survives output arriving in any other
cell.  A *structural* change -- adding, deleting, moving, splitting or
merging cells -- still clears the history, because it rebuilds the whole
buffer.

Edits made before such a change are not lost; the text is in the buffer
and in the saved notebook.  Only the record of how it got there is gone.

This file is working notes, not a specification: it records what was
measured, what the others do, and what is left.

* Measured behaviour

Typing =+ 99= into the second cell of a two-cell notebook, then asking
what undo can do:

| Situation                             | Undo entries | =C-/= does                      |
|---------------------------------------+--------------+-------------------------------|
| No redraw in between                  |            2 | undoes the typing             |
| After output arrives for ANOTHER cell |            2 | undoes the typing             |
| After a full rebuild                  |            0 | "No further undo information" |

The middle row is the case that matters in practice -- editing cell 5
while cell 1 is still running, and its plot lands -- and it used to
behave like the last row.  Making it work took two changes, and the
first alone was not enough:

1. output arriving redraws only its own cell, so most of the buffer does
   not move at all;
2. the undo entries that *did* move have their positions shifted to
   match.

Step 1 on its own left the middle row at one surviving entry and undo
still refusing, because output usually arrives from a cell ABOVE the one
being edited: the entries worth keeping are exactly the ones the new
output pushed further down.

* Why it is like this

Rendering erases the whole buffer and rebuilds it from the cell structs.
Two things follow, and the second is the awkward one.

** Recording the rebuild is worse than not recording it

The first version let the rebuild go into the undo history.  A single
redraw pushed about twenty entries, and undoing one of them tore the
notebook apart -- deleting every cell after the one being edited, because
an undo entry described a deletion of a region that, by then, meant
something else entirely.  Rendering is therefore done with
=buffer-undo-list= bound to =t=, which keeps it out of the history.

** But then the history that remains is describing a buffer that no longer exists

Emacs undo entries hold plain buffer positions -- =(BEG . END)= pairs and
bare integers -- not markers.  Nothing adjusts them when text moves
around while undo recording is off.  After a rebuild, an entry recorded
before it may point anywhere.  Replaying it does not fail loudly; it
quietly changes the wrong text.

So the choice is between losing the history and risking silent
corruption.  emjupy currently clears =buffer-undo-list= when a rebuild
actually changed the text, and leaves it alone when the text came out
identical.  Losing undo is bad.  Corrupting a notebook is worse.

* How the others avoid it

** JupyterLab

Each cell is a separate CodeMirror editor with its own history.  There is
no shared buffer for a redraw to invalidate, so the question does not
arise.  Nothing in Emacs gives this for free: a notebook here is one
buffer.

** ein

=ein= keeps the single-buffer design and does the bookkeeping. From
=ein-worksheet.el=:

- =ein:%which-cell%= is a parallel list, one entry per =buffer-undo-list=
  entry, recording which cell each change belonged to.  It is maintained
  in an =after-change-functions= hook whose own docstring opens "This is a
  costly after-change-functions hook."
- =ein:worksheet--jigger-undo-list= shifts the positions inside undo
  entries by a signed distance when a cell's length changes.  Doing that
  correctly means handling every shape an undo entry can take: integers,
  =(BEG . END)=, =(TEXT . POS)=, markers, =(nil PROP VAL BEG . END)= and
  =(apply ...)=.  The function is headed:

  #+begin_quote
  Nobody said it was easy.
  No one ever said it would be this hard.
  #+end_quote

- =buffer-undo-list= is pruned at 2000 entries, because Emacs garbage
  collects it by *bytes* and elisp cannot see that number.
- =ein:worksheet-enable-undo= covers "cell inputs only (as opposed to
  whole-cell operations such as killing, moving, executing cells)".

That last point is worth noting: even ein, having done all this work,
scopes undo to edits *within* a cell and not to structural operations.
Whatever emjupy does should aim for the same scope rather than something
more ambitious.

* What is done, and what is left

** Done: redraw only what changed

Output arriving patches the text between the end of a cell's source and
the end of its output box, leaving the rest of the buffer untouched.
Worth doing on its own merits -- it is also why a =tqdm= loop no longer
makes Emacs crawl, and why fontification no longer restarts from
scratch on every message.

The full rebuild remains for structural changes, where it is the honest
thing to do: the buffer genuinely is being replaced.

** Done: keep and shift the undo entries

Three cases, all of them in =emjupy--undo-adjust=:

- entries wholly before the edit are untouched by it and stay as they
  are;
- entries describing text inside the replaced region describe text that
  no longer exists, and go;
- entries after the region are still valid but have moved, so their
  positions shift by the size change.

The third case is the one that does the work, and the one to be careful
with.  A wrong position does not raise an error, it rewrites the wrong
text, so each undo entry shape is handled explicitly --  =(BEG . END)=,
=(TEXT . POS)= including the negative POS that means point was at the end
of the deleted text, =(nil PROP VAL BEG . END)=, and =(apply ...)=.  This
is the same job =ein= does in =jigger-undo-list=.

** Done: inserting and deleting a cell

Both draw incrementally now.  Inserting moves only what follows the new
cell, deleting moves only what followed the old one, and in each case by
a known amount -- so the same three-way treatment applies: keep what is
before, drop what described the removed text, shift what comes after.

Measured: typing in the first cell, then adding or deleting a cell at
the end, leaves the typing undoable.  It used to leave the history
empty.

** Done: split and merge

Both go through one helper with insert and delete: a split is one region
becoming two cells, a merge is two regions becoming one, and in each
case the change is bounded by the cells involved.

One wrinkle worth recording.  The check that the overlays still line up
has to happen BEFORE the new cell list is stored, because a cell just
added has no overlay yet and the check would fail on the very cell about
to be drawn.  Split fell back to a full rebuild -- silently, and only
undo showed it -- until that was moved.

** The larger alternative: leave =buffer-undo-list= out of it

Track each cell's edits in the cell struct, and give =emjupy-undo= its own
command that only ever replays within the cell at point.  This is what
JupyterLab does in effect, and it is immune to the whole class of
position bugs, since nothing is keyed by buffer position.

It means reimplementing undo and redo, including the parts people expect
without thinking about them -- redo after undo, boundaries that match
what a user considers "one change", interaction with =undo-tree= if they
use it.  Worth it only if incremental structural rendering turns out not
to be enough.

* What was swept at the end

Every operation that changes the buffer was tried in turn, typing into
one cell and then acting on another:

| Operation           | Undo afterwards |
|---------------------+-----------------|
| output arrives      | kept            |
| insert a cell       | kept            |
| delete a cell       | kept            |
| split a cell        | kept            |
| merge cells         | kept            |
| cycle cell type     | kept            |
| clear cell output   | kept            |
| re-render command   | kept            |
| two operations back to back | kept    |

Clearing output was the last one still rebuilding the notebook, and so
the last one discarding the history.  Nothing had ever been lost to it
-- text and cells came through intact -- but an edit made before
clearing could not be undone afterwards.

* Where this stands

Output arriving, inserting a cell and deleting a cell are all handled.
Split and merge are what remain, and they are the same shape of problem.
Whether to do them should be decided by how often they actually bite,
now that the common cases no longer do.
